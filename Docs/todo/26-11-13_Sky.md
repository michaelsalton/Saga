2. Fog that follows the sky
This is the biggest visual payoff for the least code. The forward pass already compiles fog variants (multi_compile_fog), and fog blends distant geometry toward RenderSettings.fogColor.

What's wrong now: that color is static. At night, distant buildings fade to a daytime grey and stand out against a dark sky.
The fix: set fogColor from the sky's horizon gradient on each step, so the far terrain melts into the horizon band.
Also worth trying: shift the fog color toward the sun's glow when looking toward it.
3. Shadow bias by sun elevation
At low sun angles you'll get acne, the speckled self-shadowing on lit surfaces. Put depth and normal bias on profile curves, the same way shadow strength already is.

4. Derive ambient from the sky
Right now you keep two parallel sets of gradients (ambient and sky) and have to keep them consistent by hand.

The sky formula is simple enough to evaluate in C# over a few dozen directions and project into the SH probe.
Ambient then comes from the sky you see, and the ambient gradients go away.
The bounce lobe stays as it is.
6. Night content
Each of these is its own feature, so only take them on if you want them:

Stars: fade in as the sun drops below about −6°.
Moon phase: right now the moon sits exactly opposite the sun, so it's always full.
Lamps: game-side hooks so lanterns and windows switch on at dusk.
Saving the clock: SagaTimeOfDay currently has no events for that or saving.
